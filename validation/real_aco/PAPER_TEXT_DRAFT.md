We conducted an exploratory ACO-only comparison on libpng 1.6.43 and zlib 1.3.1 using the baseline scheduler, a 5% uniform-exploration variant, and recency-weighted statistics combined with 5% exploration. The MLP component was disabled in all variants to isolate mutation scheduling. Each configuration was evaluated in three independent 30-second runs with identical initial corpora, a common CPU affinity, and randomized run order. The baseline here refers to the revised ACO with hierarchical feedback and global evaporation, before introducing recency and explicit exploration; it is not vanilla AFL or the original submitted implementation.

Final queue corpora were replayed against separately built GCC/gcov versions of the same libraries and drivers. Coverage counts exclude the driver and external dependencies. Median source-branch counts were 384, 378, and 380 for zlib, and 892, 895, and 892 for libpng, for baseline, exploration-only and combined variants, respectively. These short pilot measurements did not show a consistent coverage benefit for the combined mechanism, despite its improved rediscovery behavior in synthetic switching tasks. The small sample size, short budget and WSL host conditions preclude significance or broad competitiveness claims. Longer independent evaluation is required before selecting the combined policy as the final configuration.

The fuzzing build used AFL GCC, which appends its default -O3 and loop-unrolling options; the independent measurement build used GCC -O0 -g --coverage. Coverage is measured over saved final queue inputs rather than every input executed during fuzzing, and gcov source branches should not be conflated with AFL bitmap novelty or the main FuzzBench metric.
