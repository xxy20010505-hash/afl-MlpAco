We evaluated recency-weighted operator statistics with a uniform exploration mixture in three synthetic reward environments. Six variants were evaluated for 30 seeds each and 30,000 two-operator sequences per run. With a retention factor of 0.999 and exploration probability of 0.05, the combined variant rediscovered the new rewarded sequence in 30/30 runs of the original switching task, compared with 1/30 for the baseline and 16/30 for recency alone. Exploration alone also recovered in 30/30 runs, but achieved fewer successful sequences after the switch (median 359 versus 583). However, the combined variant underperformed recency alone in the stationary task and had fewer final-window successes than exploration alone in the original switching task. These mechanism-level results motivate further real-target evaluation; they do not establish universal superiority or improved program coverage. The recency module jointly changes forgetting, smoothing and update timing. Both reward levels still contribute unit success counts, so reward weighting is unchanged.
